Introduction

Ice hockey is a team sport played on ice. Each team tries to score by shooting a puck into the opponent’s goal. In this activity, we use team-level season data to ask a sports analytics question: are teams that score more goals or allow fewer goals usually more successful?

The NHL, or National Hockey League, is the major professional hockey league in North America. The KHL, or Kontinental Hockey League, is a major professional hockey league based mainly in Eurasia. These leagues are not identical in schedule, geography, roster structure, or playing style, so comparing them gives students a useful example of how data analysts make variables comparable before drawing conclusions.

This module compares NHL and KHL team performance during the 2024-25 season. The main response variable is win percentage, the proportion of games a team won. The two main explanatory variables are goals for per game and goals against per game. Goals for measures offensive production. Goals against measures defensive performance.

Students will create comparable variables, combine the two league datasets, make scatterplots, calculate correlations, and interpret the results in context. The activity is written so it can work well in an introductory statistics or data science course, including a STAT 113-level course.

NoteActivity Length

This activity is suitable for a single class period or as a short out-of-class lab.

NoteLearning Objectives

By the end of this activity, you will be able to:

  1. Create comparable performance variables across two sports datasets.
  2. Combine NHL and KHL team data into one tidy dataset.
  3. Visualize relationships between goals scored, goals allowed, and win percentage.
  4. Calculate and interpret correlations by league.
  5. Explain why correlation does not prove causation.
NoteMethods

Technology Requirement:

This activity requires R with familiarity in Quarto documents and tidyverse tools, especially dplyr, readr, and ggplot2. Students will also use correlation-based thinking to compare the strength and direction of relationships.

NoteConclusion

Through this module, students work with two real-world sports datasets and build a short analysis workflow from raw team statistics to interpretation. They begin by creating comparable variables for NHL and KHL teams, then combine the datasets into one tidy structure for league-level comparison.

The activity highlights an important idea in applied data science: comparisons are only meaningful when variables are constructed consistently across groups. By calculating win percentage, goals for per game, and goals against per game in the same way for both leagues, students create a fair basis for analysis.

The final scatterplots and correlation summaries encourage students to think carefully about how offense and defense relate to winning. Rather than relying only on general assumptions about which league is more offensive or defensive, students use data to evaluate whether goals scored and goals allowed have different relationships with win percentage in the NHL and KHL.

Students also practice interpreting correlation responsibly. The analysis can show whether variables are associated, but it cannot prove that offense or defense directly causes a team to win more games.
